# Possible Data Breach Response (Prototype Demonstration Material)

Preserve logs and access records before retention windows expire. Identify the data owner, systems, identities, time range, records accessed, export or transfer evidence, and whether confidentiality was actually affected. Do not claim a breach is confirmed until evidence supports that conclusion.

Contain the access path, revoke compromised identities or tokens, restrict exposed storage, and preserve forensic copies. Engage incident leadership, privacy, legal, and communications teams early; notification duties depend on jurisdiction, contracts, data type, and verified facts. Maintain a decision log and chain of custody. Monitor for further access while investigation and recovery proceed.

Hungarian retrieval aliases: adatvédelmi incidens, adatszivárgás, adatlopás, ügyféladat-letöltés, jogosulatlan export, érzékeny adatok, külső fiók, bejelentési kötelezettség.

## Verification questions

Distinguish public exposure, unauthorized access, attempted access, and confirmed acquisition. Identify data categories, subjects, record count, encryption state, jurisdiction, retention, recipients, and evidence of download or transfer. A successful export by an unauthorized external identity is stronger evidence than a scanner merely discovering a public URL.

## Evidence and governance

Preserve storage audit events, object and permission history, identity and token logs, export-job records, network transfer evidence, application logs, and the affected dataset's classification. Do not notify externally or state that a breach is confirmed without authorization from the organization's legal/privacy process.
